**MINDFUL MOMENT**

Yesterday we also formalized the Ponytail restraint ladder: seven rungs from "does this need to exist?" down to "minimum that works." The rule is simple — stop at the first rung that solves it. But the insight underneath it is worth sitting with. The best engineering decision you can make is often the decision not to build. Every abstraction you skip is one fewer surface area for bugs, one fewer thing to explain, one fewer thing to unlearn when the problem changes. ZAO has 296 components, 20 hooks, 42 lib domains — the codebase is already rich. The bias toward building new things when an existing thing works is a form of waste that compounds quietly. The restraint ladder is a reminder to look before you reach for a new tool.

Intention for this week: before adding, look. The answer is usually already there.
