## Draft

ZAOstock happened yesterday. First year. Real stage, real people, real music. The whole sprint — Steve Peer's emails, Stripe payment links, deal memos held in a folder for 22 days, a missing day DJ — all of it resolved or arrived at a decision point in the same week. That's the ZABAL way: imperfect execution, relentless forward motion, and the festival happens anyway.

Here's what I've been building while organizing the event. The ZAO agent fleet measured itself this week for the first time with three independent instruments. The verdict: 10% working, 90% waiting. On October 1st, 14 terminals were open across the estate. They worked for a combined 10.75 hours and waited for 218. That's not a bug — most lanes had nothing queued. But it's the number to beat. Two changes land this week: a lane that finishes its work closes its pane instead of holding it open, and the orchestrator wakes a lane only when it has something for it to do. Fewer ghost terminals. Less overhead.

The other thing that shipped: message provenance. Every message one agent sends to another now carries a basis tag — measured, relayed, inference, or mixed. A relayed approval is refused at the receiving end. This sounds small. It isn't. It's the difference between a fleet that propagates mistakes and one that stops them at the seam.

---

**MINDFUL MOMENT**

The fleet worked 10% of the time it was open. That number sounds bad. But the lanes that worked the most were the ones that were built for one job and then closed. The ones sitting at 99% waiting had nothing to do — they were holding panes waiting for a question that didn't come.

The lesson isn't efficiency. It's clarity. A lane that knows its job and closes when it's done costs nothing and produces something. A lane that stays open waiting for relevance costs 90% of its time for 10% of its output.

The festival ran the same way. Everyone who had a specific job — the DJ, the production lead, Steve's crew — showed up and delivered. The ambiguity was always in the gaps where nobody had a clear assignment.

Build specific. Close clean. Open on demand.
