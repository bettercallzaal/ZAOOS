# 2088 — Using a DAO to build the TSM community that makes the Boston-sports media

**Type:** BRAND-STRATEGY
**Topic:** Brand
**Status:** DRAFT — Zaal-initiated (2026-07-26). The focused question: **how do you use a DAO to build a community that helps you go and do media for Boston sports?** Answer: not a token to sell fans — a **community-as-content-engine**. ZAO already knows how to build on-chain communities (WaveWarZ, ZABAL, Fractal governance, Base/Farcaster, ZOE); TSM points that playbook at a sports vertical. This doc is the community mechanics; doc 2086 is the brand, doc 2087 is the ecosystem architecture (TSM's "hub" is its *own* community, built here).

---

## The one reframe that matters

**A DAO here is not a token you sell to fans. It's the machine that turns listeners into a co-creating, co-funding street team.** Most sports media is one-directional: hosts talk, audience listens, maybe replies. A DAO-community flips it — the community helps **make** the media, **distribute** it, and **fund** the access that is the brand's whole story. That directly answers the two open questions from the grill:

- *"Who is 'we'?"* → the community literally is the content team (topics, clips, picks, guests).
- *"How do you get distribution from zero?"* → owners recruit. Fans with skin in the game share, defend, and bring friends. Ownership → loyalty → free distribution.
- *"How do two guys avoid burning out at episode 8?"* → the content engine no longer rests on two people. The community de-risks consistency — the #1 pod killer.

---

## The non-negotiable design principle (brand + evidence agree)

**Free to belong. Earn your way up. Never pay your way in.**

The biggest documented failure of sports web3 (Socios/fan tokens) is that paid tokens **paywall fandom and exclude the fans who can't afford in** — commodifying a genuine emotional connection into a transaction. For TSM that would be brand suicide: your entire identity is the **priced-out, broke, never-got-a-ticket** fan. Charging for the club betrays the whole POV.

The good news: the research says the model that *actually works* is the one your brand demands anyway — **free utility tokens + token-gated community + engagement→rewards feedback loop.** So:

- **Belonging is free.** Anyone can join the club with a free membership pass (a Farcaster/Base credential — costs them nothing).
- **Status is earned, not bought.** Reputation/points come from *doing* — making clips, winning the pick'em, bringing takes — not from a wallet balance.
- **Money flows to contributors, not from fans.** The community is a place to earn a piece by building the brand, not a place to spend to belong.

This is ZAO's "everyone gets paid / loser earns" DNA, aimed at a fan base.

## What the community actually DOES for the media (the mechanics)

1. **Co-creates the content.** Members submit takes, questions, topics, "most overrated Boston athlete" rankings; the pod covers the best ones. On-chain polls (ZAO already runs these) decide episode topics, guests, power rankings. Members whose takes get featured become contributors — and bring their own audience.
2. **Is the clip street team.** The growth engine (doc 2086) is short vertical clips. Let members cut and post clips; reward the ones that land (views → points → merch/access). One episode becomes 50 community-made clips instead of 5 you made — distribution you couldn't buy.
3. **Plays the pick'em / prediction game.** Sports is prediction markets' most natural home — and this is a *direct* transfer of ZAO's WaveWarZ know-how (doc 099). Weekly Boston pick'em, "will the Pats cover," bracket challenges, on-chain leaderboard + reputation. Gives the community something to do **daily**, not weekly, and generates content (the pod covers the standings, the winners, the beefs). **Keep it points/reputation/free-to-play — not real-money gambling** (regulatory line; see risks).
4. **Funds the access arc.** This is the killer mechanic, and it's the *Krause House* model at a scrappy scale. The brand's story is "two broke fans trying to get inside." A community treasury (the kitty) can literally **fund getting in** — members vote on which game, crowdfund a live show or a press-credential push, a member wins the seat next to the hosts. The audience doesn't just watch the "get in the building" journey — **they own it and bankroll it.** No Barstool can copy a community that funded its own way into the arena.
5. **Rewards its builders.** Clippers, pick'em runners, mods, take-writers, guest-bookers earn (points → tokens → merch/access → eventual revenue share). This is the answer to "why would the community do the work" — you make it worth their time and give them a piece.
6. **Governs the fun stuff, lightly.** Fans don't want to vote on treasury mechanics; they want to vote on merch designs, "top 5 Boston sports villains," which game to fund, the next guest. Reserve governance for identity-affirming, fun decisions; keep boring ops with the core team. (ZAO's Fractal Democracy is the heavy version — TSM needs the light, loud version.)

## Never say "DAO" to the audience — translate it

The on-chain machinery is invisible backend (the ZAO way — WaveWarZ never screams "prediction-market DAO"). To a Boston sports fan you speak sports, not crypto:

| Backend (what it is) | Front door (what you call it) |
|---|---|
| DAO / community | The club · the locker room · the group chat that runs Boston |
| Membership token/NFT | Your card · season ticket · press pass |
| Governance vote | Fan vote · the group decides |
| Treasury | The kitty · the war chest |
| Points / reputation | The standings · your record |
| Contributor rewards | Getting put on · earning your spot |

If a member never has to learn the word "blockchain" to use it, you did it right.

## Sequencing & risk (do not over-build this on day one)

- **Audience first, community second.** Do not launch a DAO to 0 fans — a 12-person community can't co-create. Build a baseline audience with the pod + clips (doc 2086's 30-day plan), *then* layer the community to deepen and activate it. The DAO makes a small audience mighty; it can't manufacture one.
- **Start with the simplest three:** a free gated group + a free weekly pick'em + a free membership pass. Add the treasury/access-funding and governance only once there's a crowd that wants them.
- **Stay on the right side of the regulatory line.** Utility/reputation/access — **not** speculative financial tokens, **not** real-money gambling. Free-to-play with non-cash prizes (merch, access, credentials). This keeps you clean *and* on-brand (you're not paywalling fandom).
- **Cap concurrency (doc 2087 / MrBeast warning).** This community is real work to run. It's one live build — don't spin up a second spoke's community until this one runs itself.

## Analogs

| Model | What it proves for TSM |
|---|---|
| **Krause House DAO** (pooled crypto to buy into a pro team; bought the BIG3 Ball Hogs as a stepping stone; 1,200 members vote on real decisions) | A sports community with a concrete **"get us access"** mission mobilizes people fast. TSM's scrappy version: fund getting into games / a live show / a credential — the access arc, community-owned. |
| **Socios / fan tokens** (~100 elite clubs, $400M+ revenue — but criticized for paywalling and excluding poorer fans) | The **anti-pattern**: don't sell belonging. Free to join, earn status. The exclusion critique is fatal to a "priced-out fan" brand — avoid it and you're both cleaner and more on-brand. |
| **ZAO WaveWarZ** (on-chain prediction battles — doc 099) | The prediction/pick'em mechanic is a *direct* transfer; the know-how already exists in-house. |
| **Free-utility token-gated communities** (the model the research says works) | Engagement→rewards feedback loop; the more you participate, the more you get. This is the whole design. |
