## What "Post-Naive" Means

Named 15 October 2025 in Mozilla Foundation's new publication *Nothing Personal*, in an essay by co-matter (Severin Matusek, Nick Houde, Paloma Moniz).

The thesis:

> "The grand idealism of the free and open internet might be over for now, but in its place falls a more practical mindset. Just like Silicon Valley's entrepreneurs are entering their 'Hard Tech' era, the people building web alternatives are also entering their Post-Naive Era."

> "Instead of fighting for grand alternatives like the free and open internet for everyone, the post-naive generation builds digital infrastructure **primarily for their communities and their needs**."

They call the results **miniverses** - the deliberate opposite of metaverses. "Each of these projects carves out an island rather than trying to change the whole sea."

Four layers on which a miniverse is built differently:

| Layer | Example | What differs |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Structural** | Subvert | Collectively owned by members, not by VCs |
| **Value** | Metalabel | Built for creators to collaborate and split earnings, not compete |
| **Design** | Perfectly Imperfect | Feed built around human recommendations, not algorithmic optimisation |
| **Community** | Trust (Berlin) | Decentralised governance evolving with the community's IRL needs |

**ZAO scores on all four** and appears on none of the maps.

## Subvert: the closest thing to ZAO that exists

Austin Robey - **a Metalabel cofounder** - started Subvert in summer 2024 after Bandcamp's sale to Epic Games (2022) and then Songtradr (2023).

The reasoning, from their FAQ:

> "Bandcamp's trajectory illustrates a depressing reality of the contemporary internet: platforms position themselves as artist-friendly alternatives, only to seemingly abandon their core values and community."

The structure:

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Model | Cooperative - "owned by its membership base of artists, labels, supporters, and workers" |
| Scale | **10,000+ members** (artists, labels, supporters) |
| Musician / label membership | **FREE** |
| Supporter membership | **$100** |
| What you get | Physical zine, membership certificate, unique member number, co-op ownership, members-only forum, ability to influence platform policies and features |
| Liability | "No personal financial liability... limited to your membership contribution" |
| Status | Alpha live for members-only testing, phased opening |
| Endorsements | Charles Broskowski (Are.na founder), Matt Werth (RVNG Intl.), Alex Durlak (Idée Fixe), Lindsey Mills (Surfer Blood) |
| **Crypto** | **"Is this a crypto thing?" → "No."** |

The design lesson for ZAO independent of crypto: **free for the people who make the value, paid for the people who benefit from it.** Musicians and labels join free; supporters pay $100. ZAO's contribution economy (ZOLs, Respect) is trying to express the same asymmetry through tokens. Subvert expresses it through co-op membership classes and a zine.

That last row is the one to sit with. The most ZAO-aligned project in music - built by a Metalabel cofounder, endorsed by the Are.na founder - treats "is this crypto" as an objection to preemptively deny in its FAQ.

## The Crypto Consensus in This Movement

Three independent data points, all from 2025-2026, all from within the ecosystem ZAO would join:

1. **Mozilla / co-matter (Oct 2025):** "We thought that web3 could be a real alternative; but have come to accept that blockchains seem to be far more useful to financialize the world rather than to democratize it."
2. **Metalabel (Feb 2024, acted on through 2026):** removed blockchains entirely after three concrete failures - see [Doc 2124](../2124-metalabel-post-crypto-pivot-vs-zao-onchain-thesis/). Kept UCAN, DIDs, Ed25519, content addressing. Dropped the ledger.
3. **Subvert (2026):** FAQ answer "No."

And a fourth, from a sympathetic outside critic - Kevin Munger, writing about the same essay:

> "Crypto enthusiasts did a lot of blue-sky thinking on this problem, but as is usually the case, there's a simpler solution that doesn't require the blockchain: have some light-leak signal in order to recruit new members (generally, a podcast), and then have a paywalled, moderated internal forum (generally, Discord)."

**This is not a hostile crowd. It is ZAO's crowd.** They are values-aligned on artist ownership, anti-platform-extraction, community governance, and the rejection of growth-at-all-costs. They arrived at co-ops, statutes, and cryptography-without-chains instead of tokens.

ZAO's position is defensible - onchain governance and contribution accounting are not the consumer-payment surface that burned Metalabel - but it is **currently undocumented**, and in this ecosystem it is the first question.

## The Diagnosis They Share (and ZAO should use)

From Yancey's "Dark Forest Theory, six years in" (2025-10-22), the sharpest one-line framing anyone in this space has produced:

> **"The internet is dying on the outside but growing on the inside."**

Supporting the claim:

- **More bot content than human content is now posted, for the first time** - Dead Internet Theory meeting Dark Forest Theory
- The 2019 essay's worry has come true: as people retreat, public space fills with "extreme voices working to gain power and influence," now aided by AI
- July 2026 **Incogni** research: more than half of respondents post less and are more selective; **more than half agree "maintaining an online presence feels like work"**; leading reasons for deleting accounts are privacy/security, harassment, excessive use, and mental health; people who step away report peace, relaxation, relief - plus FOMO

The intellectual frame he uses is **James C. Scott's "hidden transcripts"** from *Domination and the Arts of Resistance*: the public transcript conforms to what the dominant group wants; the hidden transcript is what gets said offstage. Dark forests are where hidden transcripts are drafted.

He also names the limit of his own position, which is worth copying for how ZAO talks about privacy:

> "This is coming from the voice of a white guy who's used to being positively included in the system. But this presumption of safety has never been available to others."

The idea has institutional reach now: the Zilkha Gallery at Wesleyan ran **"DFT 2025,"** co-curated by artist Salim Green, framing dark forest theory around Black agency "gained through concealment" - asking how "hiding, abstraction, evasion, a refusal of visibility... facilitate freedom." Works by Rhea Dillon, Nikita Gale, Rodney McMillian.

## What This Means for ZAO

**ZAO's actual position in the map:** a music-community miniverse with a media front door (podcast, newsletter), a gated forum, a co-op-adjacent contribution economy, and an onchain governance layer nobody else in the ecosystem has. That last item is either ZAO's differentiator or its barrier to entry, depending entirely on whether ZAO can articulate why.

**The uncomfortable symmetry:** every project in this map solved "who owns the platform" with a legal or social structure - co-op (Subvert), statute (A-Corp), collective ownership (Metalabel splits). ZAO solved it with a token. The movement's implicit claim is that the legal instrument is more durable than the technical one - which is exactly Yancey's "law is law, code is an executable suggestion."

**The opportunity:** ZAO is the only project positioned to argue that onchain governance and post-naive values are compatible, with a working music community as the evidence. That is a real, publishable position. Nobody has written it.
