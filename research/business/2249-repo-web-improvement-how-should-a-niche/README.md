## Findings Synthesis

**The core play for The ZAO:** A Web3 music-creator hub competing for queries like "Web3 artist resources" must establish topical authority on the intersection of two moderately competitive niches (Web3 tooling + music creation) before link volume becomes meaningful. The strategy has two distinct phases.

**Phase 1 - Content architecture (months 1-3):** Build a pillar page for each of 3-4 parent topics (e.g., "Web3 tools for independent artists," "on-chain royalties for musicians," "NFT music marketplaces compared," "Farcaster for music creators"). Each pillar gets 4-6 cluster pages covering subtopics at the question/intent level. Internal links reinforce the topical graph. This step earns the right to rank; links then accelerate.

**Phase 2 - Earned links (months 2-6 overlapping):** The highest-ROI tactics in order of effort-to-return, based on verified evidence:
1. Original data studies about The ZAO community or Web3 music trends - these are pitchable to crypto media (Decrypt, CoinDesk), music trade press (Hypebot, Digital Music News), and creator economy publications (Creator Economy Newsletter). A single data piece can earn 20-87 links from DR 50-95 publications.
2. Expert commentary via journalist query services (HARO, Qwoted, Muck Rack) - supply quotes on Web3 music, artist royalties, or decentralized social for news cycles.
3. Unlinked brand mention reclamation - The ZAO's Farcaster activity and any existing press generates mentions; claim the links.
4. Guest bylines in music industry and Web3 trade publications - one byline per month in a niche-relevant outlet is sustainable and compounds.
5. Community resource page placements - artist resource roundups, Web3 onboarding guides, and music incubator resource pages actively seek curated tool lists.

**What not to do:** Generic directory submissions and low-relevance link exchanges do not build topical authority. A Web3/music site is harmed by off-niche links in the same way it is helped by on-niche ones.
