## What each source actually contributes

### Nightingale - the instruction, not the inspiration

From the 2026-09-25 transcript (`zao-vault/meetings/2026-09-25-the-strangest-secret-transcript.txt`, 476 lines, transcribed locally):

1. **One goal on a card.** "Make sure it's a single goal and clearly defined."
2. **Carried and read** on waking, during the day, and last thing at night.
3. **Replace, do not suppress**: "Each time a fearful or negative thought comes into your consciousness, replace it with a mental picture of your positive and worthwhile goal."
4. **Do more than you have to do**, daily, for thirty days.
5. **Do not plan the route.** "Don't concern yourself too much with how you're going to achieve your goal."

Point 5 is the one that collides with this estate and the collision is productive - see below.

### Flow - the constraint

`identity/1164` already holds the science. The single operative number for this doc is the interruption recovery cost carried in `community/1078`: **23 minutes 15 seconds** (Gloria Mark, UC Irvine), and the finding that **a 30-second interruption costs roughly 40 minutes** once recovery is counted.

## The measurement nobody had: this workflow's real interruption profile

Counted from one session, 2026-09-24 into 2026-09-25:

| Source | Count |
|---|---|
| Cross-session messages from lanes | **11** (zaoonparagraph 3, Zaostock 3, Poidhz 2, Vault 2, Wwtracker 1) |
| `AskUserQuestion` rounds put to Zaal | **8** |
| Messages Zaal sent mid-turn, while work was running | **9** |

**That is roughly 20 context switches in a working day, most of them generated by the estate built to help.** At Gloria Mark's figure the recovery cost alone exceeds seven hours, which is more hours than the day has. The number is a ceiling, not a claim about lost time - a switch you chose is cheaper than one imposed - but the direction is not in doubt.

**The estate is currently an interruption generator.** Every lane is polite about it: they queue, they mark things unsubmitted, they refuse to open pickers on unwatched panes. None of that reduces the count; it only changes the channel.

## The design, and why Nightingale's point 5 matters here

> "Don't concern yourself too much with how you're going to achieve your goal... All you have to do is know where you're going. The answers will come to you of their own accord."

Read as mysticism that is unusable. Read as an **operating instruction for delegation** it is exactly what this estate is for: name the outcome, let the machinery find the route, do not hold the route in your head. Eleven lanes ran overnight on 2026-09-24 and produced merged PRs, a corrected public claim and a restored backup without anyone holding their routes.

The failure is not that the route is delegated. It is that **the route keeps being escalated back**.

### FLOW BLOCK - the one thing to build

```
zao-flow 90          # or 25, or whatever the block is
```

- every lane's outbound message queues to `handoffs/status/<lane>.md` instead of arriving
- `zao-no-picker-guard` returns true for every pane, so no lane opens a prompt
- the todo surface renders **the card only** - one goal, nothing else
- on expiry it prints what queued while you were gone, in one list, once

Three of those four parts already exist and fire today as safety features. Only the timer and the single-card view are new.

### THE CARD - where it lives

`~/zao-vault/TODAY.md` already exists, is rewritten in place, and is open in Obsidian. The card is one line at the top of it, above every section, written by `zao-tclip card "<the one goal>"`. It is the first thing on the page Zaal already looks at, which is the only placement that satisfies "look at it several times a day" without adding a surface.
