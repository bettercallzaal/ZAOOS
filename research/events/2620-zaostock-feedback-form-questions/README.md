# 2620 - Best questions for the ZAOstock 2026 feedback form

> **Goal:** A ranked set of 10 questions (about 2 minutes) for the ZAOstock 2026 Google Form, with exact wording, type, required or not, why each earns its slot, which ones feed the advisor's expected-vs-actual comparison, a required quote-consent question, and a separate short form for artists, crew and partners.

**Event under review:** ZAOstock, free outdoor music festival, Franklin Street Parklet, Ellsworth, Maine, Sat 3 Oct 2026. Seven acts (The Crown Vics, OPEN X, Grass Rug, Michael Anderson, DCoop, LyonsDen, Tom Fellenz), livestreamed, after-party at Black Moon. Attendance is Zaal's own estimate of about 200 visitors; there is no gate count and no stream-viewer count, so no response rate can be computed (UNKNOWN, not zero).

**Question numbers in this document (Q1 to Q10) are form order, not rank.** **Form order in plain terms:** role, overall, vs expectation, how long, grid, who you caught, do differently, best part, come again, consent plus optional contact. Easy taps first, then the grid, then the two text boxes, then the consent tap.

**Time estimate (not measured):** about 5 seconds per one-tap item, about 25 seconds for the seven-row grid, about 20 seconds per text box that is answered. In-person path with both text boxes answered is about 110 seconds; with both skipped about 70 seconds. That supports "about 2 minutes" for most people. Vendor data runs slower (Eventbrite quoting SurveyMonkey: a 20-question survey takes about 8 minutes, so 10 questions averages about 4). The honest claim is "2 minutes if you skip the text boxes, about 3 if you write in both". Time two people on their phones before sending.

## Which questions feed the advisor's expected-vs-actual comparison

The advisor wants "what actually happened compared with what we expected" and "what we want to carry forward, change, or build on". A guest form measures only the "actual" and the guest's perception of "expected". The real expected column has to come from the organisers, written down before anyone reads the responses.

| Advisor item (from the scope file) | Guest question | Insider question | Where "expected" comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Q5 grid row 1 | - | Phase A pinch-point and dead-zone list (owed per the scope file; not seen by this lane), else Zaal's own pre-event notes |
| Stopping, watching | Q4 (how long), Q5 row 2 | - | Phase A guest-decision moments |
| Sitting | Q5 row 3 | - | Phase A list; seating count from the layout file |
| Restrooms, shelter | Q5 rows 4 and 5 | - | Phase A list |
| Business interfaces | Q5 row 6 | B6 | Phase A list |
| The 6 PM move to Black Moon | Q5 row 7, Q6 (after-party option) | - | Run of show |
| Overall perception vs plan | Q3 | B2 | Q3 and B2 are the only items that carry an expectation inside the answer |
| What actually happened (difference) | Q3 with the Q7 text | B3 | AAR step 2 |
| Carry forward | Q8 (best part) | B4 | AAR "what went well and why" |
| Change | Q7 (do differently) | B5 | AAR "what can be improved and how" |
| Build on | Q9 (come again), Q6 (acts caught) | B7 | Sustain-or-improve decision |

**Method note from the AAR sources:** the four questions are "What was expected to happen? What actually occurred? What went well and why? What can be improved and how?" (USAID guide, CEBMA guide), and the first one is answered "beginning with the charter or other project planning documents", not from memory after the event. So the expected column is filled from the run of show and Phase A, then the form fills the actual column.

**Complement, not duplicate:** the advisor's Phase B capture sheet (volunteers, event day) measures what staff saw. This form measures what guests report. Where the two disagree is the finding.

## Findings

### Survey length and completion

| Source | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Eventbrite (quoting SurveyMonkey) | A maximum of 10 questions "will ensure the highest completion rates"; completion drops by 5% to 20% past 7 or 8 minutes; 20 questions takes about 8 minutes | Vendor guidance |
| SurveyMonkey (2026) | "10 questions or fewer, which most people can finish in about three minutes"; send within one business day; up to two reminders within two weeks | Vendor guidance |
| Family Arts toolkit | Maximum 6 questions for "off-the-cuff, public surveys and exit surveys", 10 for workshop participants, 20 for people who agreed to in-depth evaluation | Arts-sector guidance, funder-aligned |
| VenueSight vs Survicate | 8 questions about 40 to 50% completion, 15 questions 25 to 30% (VenueSight); 1 to 3 questions 83% (Survicate) | **Contradiction and weak:** both are vendor blogs with no stated dataset. Use as direction only |

**What it means here:** a free public festival is closest to the Family Arts "public survey" row (6 questions) for walk-ups, and to the "participant" row (10) for people who were invited by link after the fact. Respondents arriving through a link from a recap, a newsletter or a poster are self-selected, so 10 is defensible and 6 would be safer. The cut order is the optional hear-about item, then Q6 (who you caught), then Q8 (best part), then Q4 (how long), which gets a respondent to 6 or 7 questions.

### Question design

- **Label every scale point.** The general consensus is that fully labeled scales produce better data quality than endpoint-only scales; reliability .719 labeled vs .506 endpoint-only in one comparison (Maitland, Survey Practice, citing Alwin 2007). It is easy for a 5-point scale and hard past 5 or 6 points.
- **Open text last.** More respondents left when open-ended questions came first, and withdrawal was highest on smartphones with large text boxes; bigger boxes get longer answers (Yamazaki et al., OzCHI 2023). That is a trade, so use one paragraph box (Q7) and one short box (Q8).
- **No double-barrelled items.** "How was the venue and food?" should be two questions (GCFA); "Rate the sound, lighting, and stage production" should be three (Big Tickets). The grid has one idea per row.
- **Forced text answers drive people away.** HN comment: "The second was a free text response field ... It was a mandatory question ... So I just closed the window and never completed the survey." (Community source; anecdote.)
- **Short beats complete.** HN comment on "Mistakes startups make when talking to users": "They forgot the #1 mistake: Too Many Damn Questions." Same anecdotal weight; consistent with the vendor sources.
- **Mobile.** A mobile-survey experiment (Sage, "Experiments in Mobile Web Survey Design") found the small screen and keyboard "introduce undesirable differences in responses" (abstract only). The grid is the one risk: Google Forms renders a grid as a vertical stack per row on a phone, so test it on a phone.

### NPS vs plain satisfaction (comparison)

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict for ZAOstock 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| NPS, 0 to 10 "likely to recommend" | One number, familiar to sponsors; Swoogo: tells you "whether your attendees are likely to come back"; EMMA: credible as an event-loyalty indicator when asked of all attendees of an event that repeats | 11-point scale cannot be labeled (Maitland); Swoogo: "the most valuable benchmark is your own event history", and there is none; EMMA: "was NOT developed as a brand perception metric"; with about 20 to 40 responses one answer moves the score by several points | SKIP this year. Revisit once there are two editions to compare |
| Labeled 1-5 overall (CSAT-style) | Labeled, fast, readable at n=25 as a count | Not comparable to other events | USE (Q2) |
| "Compared with what you expected" 5-point | Carries the expectation inside the answer, answers the advisor directly | Hindsight colors expectations | USE (Q3) |
| "Would you come again" Yes/Maybe/No | Intent in plain words, no benchmark needed | Coarse | USE (Q9) |

### Required vs optional

Zaal's rule (consent required) plus the evidence above give: required = role, overall, consent. Everything else optional. Google Forms also lets a respondent submit with no answer on optional items, which keeps the grid usable ("Did not use or notice" is the opt-out).

### Segmentation and the livestream audience

- GCFA: "If different groups had different roles, schedules, or goals, they should not all receive the exact same survey." SurveyMonkey: send sponsor questions "only to exhibitors and sponsors so you can track partnership satisfaction separately from attendee feedback". Culture Counts (Impact & Insight festivals guide): "Create separate surveys within one evaluation tailored to different groups."
- Hybrid reporting: Sopact: "Do not average in-person and virtual ratings ... Report each group with its own response base, and compare only the questions both groups saw." That is why Q1 is a single-choice branch and why Q4 and Q5 each have a street version and a stream version with the same shape.
- Eventbrite: keep at least one segmentation question. Big Tickets: tenure (first time vs returning) is "one of the most valuable cuts"; for a first edition every respondent is a first-timer, so that question is skipped.

### Festival and free-event precedents

- Americans for the Arts, Arts & Economic Prosperity 6: audience-intercept survey of 13 questions (7 on spending, 1 social-impact scale, 5 demographic and optional), 224,677 valid surveys, 68% paper and 32% electronic by QR code. It measures spending and impact, not guest experience, so it is a method precedent for short, anonymous, optional-demographics design, not a question source.
- Maine Arts Commission, Maine Performing Arts Festivals Economic Impact Study 2011 (15 festivals, 1,684 usable responses): a Maine precedent that festival audiences are surveyed on-site by questionnaire. Spending-focused. [PARTIAL: search extract only, the PDF returned 403 to curl.]
- American Folk Festival, Bangor 2008 (USDA research paper): on-site stage of 953 questionnaires, then an online follow-up to 602 collected emails, 314 completed, 52% response. Evidence that collecting an email on the spot roughly halves the follow-up gap; for ZAOstock the email field is optional on the form.
- Mass Cultural Council festivals toolkit: send "immediately following the festival and close it within two weeks"; ask who they are, how they found out, what interested them.
- Family Arts toolkit: "I am happy to be quoted anonymously" as an end tick-box; QR code links for people without contact details.
- Culture Counts: display a QR code at the exit, the information point and the tea tent. For ZAOstock the same idea is a QR code on the replay page and the recap, since the event is over.

### What the form cannot do

- It cannot say how many guests were there: the 200 is an estimate and there is no gate count; no response rate can be reported (UNKNOWN). Report "N responses" and the group, nothing more.
- It reaches self-selected people. The HN "Quiet Collapse of Surveys" and "survey fatigue" threads make the same point: the people who answer are not a random sample. Treat the form as a source of reasons and ideas, and the Phase B sheet and Zaal's own notes as the source of what happened.
- Retrospective "expected" is perception, not a measured plan.

## Contradiction and staleness checks

- **Contradiction:** vendor completion figures disagree (see table above). No neutral dataset was found. The decision does not hinge on a figure: all sources agree shorter is better, and 10 or fewer is the common ceiling.
- **Contradiction:** NPS sources split between "gold standard" (Swoogo, InEvent) and "credible only in two narrow uses" (EMMA). Resolved by the decision to skip NPS this year, not by picking a winner.
- **Staleness:** SurveyMonkey page dated 2026-04-29, Swoogo 2026-03-25, EMMA 2024-04-29, Eventbrite 2023-02-09, Maitland 2009 (method findings, not price or version facts), AEP6 2023. All checked 2026-10-06.
