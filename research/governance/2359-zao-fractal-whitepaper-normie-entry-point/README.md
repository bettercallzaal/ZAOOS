## The one-paragraph version

Every week, small groups of ZAO members sit down and rank each other on who
did the most for the community that week - not by vote, by talking it out
until everyone agrees on the order. The person the group ranks highest gets
the most "Respect" - a kind of on-chain reputation that can't be bought,
sold, or given away, only earned. Respect decides who has a real say in
ZAO's decisions. Nobody can buy their way into power here, because there's
nothing to buy.

## Why not just vote normally?

Most online communities that try to govern themselves with tokens run into
the same problem: whoever has the most money ends up with the most power,
and almost nobody bothers to vote because one vote among thousands doesn't
matter. ZAO does something different on purpose. Instead of one huge vote
where your voice barely counts, you're in a small group - five or six
people - where your voice is a fifth or a sixth of the room. You have to
actually show up, actually contribute, and actually convince the people who
know your work. That's a much higher bar than clicking a button, and it's
the whole point: it makes the people with power the people who earned it.

This idea isn't new. It's called "fractal governance," and ZAO didn't
invent it - the theory comes from a researcher named Daniel Larimer, and
the specific on-chain tools ZAO uses were built by a team called
Optimystics. What ZAO built is the first version of this run inside a music
community, week after week, for over two years straight.

## How it actually works, in five steps

1. **Every Monday**, ZAO members split into small groups.
2. **Each group talks it out** - no ballots, just conversation - until they
   agree on who did the most that week, second-most, and so on.
3. **Points get handed out** based on that ranking. First place gets the
   most, last place still gets something. Those points are called
   "Respect."
4. **Respect is permanent and personal.** It lives on the blockchain, tied
   to your wallet, and nobody can take it, buy it, or transfer it to
   someone else.
5. **Respect determines your say** in what ZAO decides - not your wallet
   balance, not how long you've been around, what you've actually
   contributed, as judged by the people who watched you contribute it.

## What makes ZAO's version different

Nobody else has run this for a creative community. Every other place doing
this kind of governance is focused on funding, protocol decisions, or
general DAO business. ZAO built its ranking criteria around music, art, and
community - the questions each group asks are things like "who pushed the
music forward this week" and "who helped a newcomer get started," not "who
voted the right way on a treasury proposal." And ZAO has done this every
single week, without a break, since August 2024 - longer than almost any
other community running this kind of governance, anywhere.

## What ZAO does NOT claim

This matters as much as what it does claim. ZAO does not pretend this
system is perfect or finished:

- **A handful of wallets currently finalize on-chain actions.** That's a
  real concentration of power the community is working to spread out, not
  something we're pretending isn't there.
- **Some ideas from the original design aren't turned on yet.** For
  example, the original theory says Respect should slowly fade over time
  so it always reflects recent contribution, not just history. ZAO doesn't
  do that today - Respect currently just adds up forever. Whether to turn
  that on is an open community proposal, not a decided fact, and this
  document will never say it's live when it isn't.
- **This hasn't been proven at huge scale.** It works well for a
  few hundred people. Nobody - not ZAO, not anyone else running this kind
  of governance - has proven it works for tens of thousands.
