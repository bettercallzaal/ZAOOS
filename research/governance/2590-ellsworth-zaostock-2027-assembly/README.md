# 2590 - ZAOstock 2027 as the Annual Assembly of ZAO Ellsworth

> **Goal:** Turn Zaal's 2026-10-05 ruling (anyone present can vote; ZAOstock 2027 visitors can come to vote) into a design: what a festival-day assembly votes on, how check-in and counting work for hundreds of voters, how it resists a bloc traveling in, and what the monthly local meetings handle.

Builds on [doc 2589](../2589-ellsworth-chapter-in-person-vote/) (merged as PR 3717). The ruling is recorded in vault `decisions/grill-2026-10-05-zao-ellsworth-idea.md` (read on zao-vault origin/main, 2026-10-05). Doc 2589 Q1 and Q2 are now answered: presence is the only requirement.

## Key Decisions / Recommendations

| # | Decision | Recommendation | Why |
|---|----------|----------------|-----|
| 1 | Structure | **BUILD two tiers.** Monthly Ellsworth meetings run chapter business and small money. One annual ZAOstock Assembly, on festival day (target Sat 18 Sep 2027), decides the big questions: charter, officers, the annual budget and any spend above a cap. | Cooperatives with festivals do exactly this: one big yearly vote, many small boards between (Midcounties, OPALCO below). It also gives the festival a reason beyond the lineup. |
| 2 | Agenda notice | **PUBLISH the assembly agenda 60 days ahead (by Sat 19 Jul 2027), proposals close 45 days ahead, and ban new charter or money motions from the floor.** Monthly meetings keep the 10-day warrant from doc 2589. | The main defense against a traveling bloc is that nobody can spring a motion. Maine's own town-meeting law uses a 90-day lead for changes to how officials are chosen (30-A MRSA 2525 and 2528). |
| 3 | Capture defense | **USE five layers**: long notice, two-thirds for charter and money, a quorum floor that scales with turnout, a 14-day cooling-off, and ratification at the next monthly meeting by a reject-only rule. | No single layer stops a bloc; each raises its cost. Details in section 3. |
| 4 | Two-meeting rule | **CHANGE doc 2589's rule from "the voter must have attended an earlier meeting" to "the motion must pass two meetings".** | The voter-level version excludes exactly the first-time ZAOstock visitor Zaal wants to welcome. See Q2 below. |
| 5 | Check-in | **RUN a two-step check-in**: voter wristband and name on a paper roll at a desk (open the day before and from 10:00 on the day), then one ballot card per wristband at the assembly room door. ID glance, no wallet needed. | Mirrors the ICA General Assembly practice of picking up badges and voting materials the day before, signing an attendance sheet and showing legal ID. Splitting the steps keeps the room door fast. |
| 6 | Counting | **USE one paper ballot card per voter with all motions on it, counted in 4 piles by pairs of tellers, reconciled to the wristband count.** Results read on stage at a posted time (target 90 minutes after the room closes). | Paper is the only count that cannot be forwarded or spoofed (doc 2589). The wristband count gives a hard cross-check (ballots cast must not exceed wristbands issued). |
| 7 | Incentives | **GIVE every attendee the same perks (voter wristband free with entry, a stamp card, a raffle open to everyone who attended the assembly). Do NOT reimburse travel for voting, and do NOT make perks depend on how you vote.** | OPALCO runs a raffle for members who attended and for members who voted. Anything that pays for votes or for travel to vote invites the bloc this doc is trying to blunt. |
| 8 | People who cannot travel | **LET them propose, comment, watch the stream and join a labeled advisory straw poll, and nothing else.** Fix everything fixable for people who do come (access, seating, captions, a quiet room). | Keeps Zaal's rule intact; the Maryland and Co-operatives UK examples show hybrid votes exist, and the chapter chose against them on purpose. |
| 9 | Pilot ladder | **TREAT the 14 Nov 2026 pilot and the monthly meetings that follow as rehearsal.** Run a 50-person mock assembly in Aug 2027 with the exact desks and ballots. | The big day is one shot a year; a failed count at 300 people is the most likely way to lose trust. |

## Findings

### 1. Precedents for gatherings that double as binding votes

| Precedent | What it shows | Source and evidence level |
|---|---|---|
| Midcounties Co-operative, AGM at the Fairer Living Festival (Bescot Stadium, Walsall) | The AGM "formed part of the Fairer Living Festival" and in-person attendees were "encouraged to enjoy the events and activities scheduled through the day." 248 members in person, 26 online, 11 guests. A test vote was taken first; voting happened at the end; the meeting closed at 12.00pm. | Minutes read in full (pdftotext). Hybrid, so it is a precedent for the festival-plus-AGM shape, not for banning online votes. |
| OPALCO (San Juan County, Washington), annual member festival | The business meeting and the festival are separate days. 2026: 55 community members at the virtual business meeting; the festival the next day had about 200. 2025: 24 members at the business meeting, over 350 at the festival; 1,801 ballots (15.4% of membership) cast by ballot. The festival raffle was open to members "who attended the event and members who voted." | Press releases read. Lesson: the festival drew 7 to 15 times more people than the business meeting did, so putting the vote inside the festival, not beside it, is what changes turnout. Also a lesson on incentives: raffle for voting is normal practice at a co-op. |
| ICA General Assembly 2023 (Brussels) | Members "pick up their badges and voting materials" the day before (16:00 to 18:00) and arrive 30 minutes early to sign the attendance sheet; present legal ID; sign for voting materials; none after voting starts; motions by show of voting cards unless a secret ballot is requested; written amendments 7 days ahead; two voting surveyors and a bureau; proxies allowed up to 12 votes each. | Official documentation PDF, search highlights plus the quoted text (PARTIAL, whole PDF not read). Closest model for the check-in and for the amendment deadline. The proxy rule is the opposite of what the chapter wants. |
| Maryland cooperative law (SB 561, 2021) | One member one vote; quorum for a co-op of up to 1,000 members is 5% of members attending; 50 attending for a larger one; a person may carry at most 3 proxies. | Bill text, search highlights (PARTIAL). Shows quorum scaling is standard and that the cap on proxies exists because bloc proxy collection is a known abuse. |
| Maine town meeting (30-A MRSA 2524, 2525, 2528) | Moderator elected first by written ballot; moderator presides; "A person who is not a voter in the town may speak at the meeting only with the consent of 2/3 of the voters present"; officials cannot be elected "on a motion to cast one ballot"; changes to which officials are elected need a meeting at least 90 days before the annual meeting; a secret ballot takes precedence over any other vote at the same meeting. | Statute pages fetched raw with curl (FULL). Borrow: moderator-first order, the 2/3 rule for visitors speaking (invert it for ZAO: visitors may vote, so speaking time needs its own rule), the 90-day lead, and a secret ballot overriding a hand vote. Note: Maine has no statewide "Town Meeting Day" like Vermont's first-Tuesday-in-March; each town warrants its own date, so there is no fixed Maine logistics template to copy. |
| Zuzalu, Edge City and similar pop-up villages | Treated as governance experiments, not binding assemblies. A resident post lists Zupoll for polling and notes a bottleneck where the community waited on one founder's approval; Edge City founders call governance "a playground". | Blog posts and news (PARTIAL, highlights). Lesson: these events test voting tools but did not run binding in-person votes with fixed rules; the lesson is the founder bottleneck, which a published agenda and clerk-run tally avoids. |
| Optimism, Gitcoin | Both vote online (Optimism onchain and Citizens' House; Gitcoin Snapshot plus Tally). The searches found no in-person binding vote at any of their events. | Search results only (PARTIAL). Treat as a negative signal: no in-person onchain-community assembly with binding votes was found in three searches. Burning Man's "Cultural Direction Setting" used a survey (4,804 responses from 78 countries) and 19 community conversations, which is consultation, not binding vote. |
| Doc 2589's AlloIRL, POAP Vote and Birdbrain examples | Already in doc 2589 | Reuse the AlloIRL lessons: walk-ins who are not on a list need a path; test the reset. |

Not found: a festival that runs a binding in-person vote open to all attendees regardless of membership or residence. Every precedent above restricts voting to members or residents. ZAO Ellsworth's "anyone present" rule has no direct precedent in this search, which makes the capture defenses below load-bearing, not decorative. Searched: Exa (2 of 3 queries ran; the third hit Exa's free rate limit), WebSearch (2 queries), curl on Maine statutes and Midcounties minutes. Not searched: Reddit, Hacker News, New England Town Meeting Day press (Vermont), Edge City governance session outcomes in primary form, ETHDenver governance sessions in primary form.

### 2. Two tiers: monthly meetings vs the annual assembly

| Item | Monthly Ellsworth meeting | ZAOstock Assembly (annual) |
|---|---|---|
| Who votes | Anyone present at roll call, one person one vote (same rule) | Same |
| Typical size | Pilot quorum 9 (doc 2589); expected tens | Hundreds; ZAOstock 2026 targeted 200 to 300 attendance (doc 1326/1337); the actual 2026 count is not recorded in the post-event template, which still reads "[FILL]" |
| Agenda notice | 10 days | 60 days; proposals close 45 days; no new charter or money motions from the floor |
| Decides | Event dates, venues, volunteer roles, spend under the cap, ratification checks, next agenda | Charter and bylaws, officers (Moderator, Clerk, Tally Teller terms), the annual chapter budget, any single spend above the cap, the big questions (for example whether to host in 2028) |
| Vote threshold | Simple majority of those present; two-thirds for spend over the cap | Two-thirds for charter, budget and spend over the cap; simple majority for officers |
| Effect | Immediate for small items | Takes effect after cooling-off and ratification (section 3) |
| Online people | Propose, comment, watch, advisory poll | Same, plus the stream |

Why two tiers and not one: a single big vote a year gives bad feedback loops (a mistake lives 12 months) and puts all capture risk on one day. Monthly meetings keep the chapter alive between assemblies and are where locals build the relationships that make the assembly's outcomes stick. Why not only monthly: the ruling says visitors come to vote at ZAOstock; the festival is where hundreds of people physically are.

### 3. Capture risk and the layers that keep it open

The risk, stated plainly: residence is no longer required, so a bloc of B people traveling in beats L locals if B is greater than L on a simple-majority question. Under a two-thirds rule the bloc needs to be more than twice the size of everyone else combined. Presence is cheap to buy; notice and process are not.

| Layer | Rule | What it stops | Cost to open participation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Long notice | Agenda published 60 days ahead; proposals close 45 days ahead; no floor motions on charter or money | A surprise motion carried by whoever shows up with a plan | None for a genuine visitor: anyone can see the agenda and decide whether to come |
| B. Supermajority | Two-thirds for charter, budget, and spend over the cap | A bare-majority bloc | A good proposal has to win broadly, which is the point |
| C. Quorum that scales | Quorum is the larger of 50 voters present or 10% of wristbands issued; below quorum, the motion rolls to the next monthly meeting | A small-room surprise (a bloc that waits until the afternoon after most people leave) | None |
| D. Cooling-off | Charter and above-cap money decisions take effect 14 days after the assembly | Rushed regret; gives time to review the tally and any challenge | None |
| E. Ratification | The next monthly meeting (open to anyone present, same rule) can overturn an assembly decision only by two-thirds of those present, within 30 days | A bloc decision the locals would reject | Locals could in theory veto visitors, which cuts against the ruling. Reject-only with a 2/3 bar limits that. See Q2. |

Other measures:

- **Aggregate disclosure.** Record each voter's town or country on the roll for transparency, publish counts in aggregate after the vote, never names. Not an eligibility test. A visible out-of-state surge is a signal, not a disqualifier.
- **Bloc detection at sign-in.** If more than 20 voters sign in within 15 minutes with the same town and group affiliation, the Moderator may call a 10-minute recess and read the rule: one person, one vote, in person, voting your own view. Do not turn them away.
- **Visitor speaking.** Maine town meeting requires 2/3 of voters to let a non-voter speak. For ZAO, everyone in the room may speak under the Moderator's time rule (2 minutes each, as the ICA assembly uses), but a speaker cannot yield time to another.
- **Two-meeting rule resolved.** Doc 2589 said motions that spend money or change the charter need "voters who signed in at one earlier meeting." That would turn away a first-time ZAOstock visitor. This doc changes it to the motion: charter and above-cap money must pass two meetings (the assembly, then ratification at the next monthly meeting). The voter rule is dropped for the assembly. Needs Zaal's ruling (Q2).
- **Wristband not wallet.** Wallet-based gates can be sold or delegated; a person in the room with a wristband cannot be forwarded (doc 2589).

### 4. Throughput: check-in and counting for hundreds of voters

All rates below are planning assumptions, not measurements. Replace them with the August 2027 mock assembly numbers (Next Actions).

**Check-in model.** Assume 6 voters per minute per desk (name on roll, ID glance, wristband). Desks split by surname A-F, G-L, M-R, S-Z and a walk-in desk for new names.

| Voters | Desks | Minutes at 6 per desk per minute |
|---|---|---|
| 150 | 3 | 8 |
| 300 | 4 | 13 |
| 500 | 6 | 14 |
| 800 | 8 | 17 |

Check-in is open the day before for 2 hours (as ICA does) and from 10:00 on festival day until the room closes at 12:30, so peak load is spread over about 4.5 hours rather than 15 minutes.

**Voting.** Ballot cards are handed out at the room door against the wristband (one card, then the wristband is clipped or marked so it cannot be reused). Cards are collected in sealed boxes in the room. The Moderator closes the room at a posted time; the Clerk reads the wristband count issued and the card count handed out.

**Counting.** One card, all motions (up to 5) with box marks for yes, no, abstain. Cards are split into 4 piles; each pair of tellers tallies every motion for its pile on a tally sheet, then the four sheets are summed by the Clerk. Planning assumption: 25 cards a minute per pair, so 300 cards in 4 piles of 75 take about 3 minutes plus 10 for reconciliation, versus about 12 minutes for 1 pair on the full set. Disputes: any 3 voters may request a recount of any motion; the Moderator names a second pair of tellers.

**Integrity checks.** (a) cards counted must not exceed cards handed out; (b) cards handed out must not exceed wristbands issued; (c) spoiled cards go in a separate bag, counted and logged; (d) tally sheets signed by two tellers, the Clerk and the Moderator; (e) the card count and motion tallies are posted publicly with a photo of each signed sheet.

**Attestations.** After the count, the Clerk may issue EAS attestations on Base for attendance and for the minutes hash, as in doc 2589. Wallet optional; paper remains the legal record. EAS contract address in doc 2589 is still unconfirmed against official EAS docs.

**Day-of timeline (draft, festival day).**

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| Day before, 16:00 to 18:00 | Pre-check-in desk open (wristbands and roll) |
| 10:00 | Check-in desks open at the festival gate |
| 11:30 | Assembly room opens; ballot cards handed at the door |
| 12:00 | Moderator elected first, as in 30-A MRSA 2524; test vote (Midcounties did one) |
| 12:15 to 13:15 | Motions: 3 minutes per side, 2 minutes per speaker, online comments read by the Moderator |
| 13:15 | Room closes; cards boxed; count begins while music continues |
| 14:45 | Results read from the stage; signed tallies photographed and posted |

### 5. Accessibility and people who cannot travel

| Group | What they can do | What they cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| Online, anywhere | Propose (the doc 1568 template), comment, watch the livestream, join the advisory straw poll (closes one hour before the room, shown to the room, never added to the tally) | Vote |
| In Ellsworth but with a disability | Full voting; step-free room, seating, captioning on the stream and room screen, a quiet room, an aide in the room | Nothing excluded by design. Whether an aide may carry a ballot for a voter who cannot enter the room is a counsel question (Q5) |
| Under 18 | Attend where venue allows, propose, join the straw poll | Vote (age 18 or older, doc 2589) |
| Cannot afford travel | Same as online; see housing pool below | Be reimbursed for travel to vote |

The honest cost: the rule excludes people who cannot come. Accept it as Zaal's chosen trade-off, say so on every notice, and make the online lane meaningful: every online proposal that gets 3 endorsements goes on the agenda, and the Moderator reads the top 3 online comments per motion.

### 6. How the assembly can draw people to ZAOstock 2027

| Lever | Detail | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| A reason to be there | The pitch becomes "come to ZAOstock, help decide what ZAO Ellsworth does next." OPALCO's festival drew about 200 to 350 people while its business meeting drew 24 to 55, so the festival is the draw and the vote is the hook that gives it civic meaning | If the vote is dull, it becomes the boring part of the day; keep it to 90 minutes |
| Same perks for everyone | Voter wristband free with entry; stamp card; raffle for attendees; a "Founding Assembly 2027" collectible that is the same for all | Per-vote prizes invite a bloc |
| Housing pool, not subsidy | A board of hosts offering free beds and floors to visitors, coordinated through the chapter | Liability and vetting; needs a form and a rule (Q8) |
| Early agenda as marketing | The 60-day agenda is also the content for newsletter and socials: "three questions for 2027" | None |
| Online-to-IRL pipeline | Online proposers get a seat on the agenda in person if they come | Do not promise a vote to anyone who stays away |
| Ticketing | Doc 1326 weighed free RSVP versus paid tickets; the ruling says anyone physically present can vote. Whether a paid ticket or an RSVP is needed to enter the festival, and whether the assembly room is open without a ticket, is undecided (Q1) | If the room needs a ticket, ability to pay becomes a gate; if not, anyone walking by can vote |

### 7. Law and records caveats (unchanged from doc 2589, with one addition)

Maine's nonprofit act lets members vote by proxy unless the bylaws forbid it (13-B 604, per doc 2589), so the bylaws must ban proxies, mail and electronic votes on binding motions, and set the quorum and the 2/3 thresholds in writing. New for a festival-size assembly: venue capacity and any permit conditions (insurance, fire marshal count) apply to the assembly room; the Clerk's sign-in sheet is a records issue (store with the Clerk, post counts only); and a 500-person room is a different permit and insurance question than a 25-person meeting (doc 1464 covers ZAOstock insurance). Not legal advice; one counsel call covers all of it.

## Proposal: what ZAOstock 2027 votes on, how it runs, what monthly meetings do

| # | Item | Proposal (all defaults, changeable) |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | Date and shape | Sat 18 Sep 2027 in Ellsworth, a 90-minute voting block inside the festival day; room closes 13:15, results read on stage by 14:45. |
| 2 | Voting items | (a) Annual chapter budget (and any single spend over the cap); (b) election of Moderator, Clerk, Tally Teller terms; (c) charter or bylaw amendments queued through the monthly meetings; (d) up to two "big questions" chosen by the monthly meetings by 15 Aug 2027 (example: hosting ZAOstock 2028, partner choices). Maximum 5 motions per ballot. |
| 3 | Notice | Agenda published 60 days ahead (19 Jul 2027); proposals close 45 days ahead (4 Aug 2027); no floor motions on charter or money; online proposals need 3 endorsements. |
| 4 | Who votes | Anyone 18 or older physically present who signs the roll, one person one vote; no proxies, no remote, no weighting by Respect or ZOLs. |
| 5 | Check-in | Wristband plus paper roll (name, ID glance, town or country) at 4 to 6 desks open the day before and from 10:00 on the day; one ballot card per wristband at the room door; walk-in desk for new names. |
| 6 | Counting | One paper card with up to 5 motions; sealed boxes; 4 piles counted by pairs of tellers; cards counted must not exceed cards handed out, which must not exceed wristbands issued; signed sheets photographed and posted; any 3 voters can ask for a recount. |
| 7 | Safeguards | Two-thirds for charter, budget and spend over the cap; quorum is the larger of 50 voters or 10% of wristbands issued; 14-day cooling-off; the next monthly meeting can overturn only by two-thirds within 30 days. |
| 8 | Monthly meetings | Held in Ellsworth, open to anyone present. Run venues, dates, volunteers, spend under the cap, charter drafts queued for the assembly, the ratification check after assembly, and the shortlist of big questions. Pilot Sat 14 Nov 2026 per doc 2589. |
| 9 | Online people | Propose, comment, watch the stream, join the advisory straw poll; never vote. Say so on every notice. |
| 10 | Incentives | Same perks for every attendee; no travel reimbursement for voting; a volunteer housing pool; nothing tied to how you vote. |
